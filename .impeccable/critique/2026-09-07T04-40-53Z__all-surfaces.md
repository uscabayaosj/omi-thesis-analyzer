## Design Specificity Verdict

Authored, not assembled. Vocabulary and defaults carry the product: "Open promises" glossed "Whoever has waited longest is first"; "Let go" as a way to retire a promise without marking it done; "Day closed. 3 promises carried into tomorrow." The visual system matches — a copper radial pool at `50% -8%` plus an inline-SVG fractal grain, reasoned so the Flat Field Rule survives; a Tailwind remap that rethemes the app from one `@theme` block.

Structurally same-y: twelve of thirteen surfaces are the identical wrapper. `/usage` gets the same ceremonial header as `/rollup`.

Deterministic scan: `detect.mjs --json src/app src/components` → exit 0, zero findings. A `--no-config` control run produced 18 `gray-on-color` warnings, all false positives (the rule cross-pairs the two mutually-exclusive branches of one ternary class string). B's independent in-browser contrast pass found zero failures at those locations.

Both assessments independently found the single real contrast failure: `src/app/page.tsx:1788` rendered the ignored-conversation reason in `text-slate-500` (#7a6b58) at 12px — 3.59:1 against Night Pasture, the only violation of the Two Greys Rule in the codebase.

Mechanical baseline otherwise excellent: zero shadows, zero fixed heading steps, zero TODOs, zero app-origin console errors across 10 routes, zero failed network requests across 480, zero horizontal overflow at 375px, exactly one `<h1>` per route with no heading skips, zero unnamed controls, visible focus on all 11 tab stops.

## Overall Impression

Well-made, clearly through several craft passes, and the passes left their reasoning in the code — `globals.css` documents three successive failures of its own 44px rule and why each fix works. Biggest opportunity: the app's own best material is unused as structure. The ADHD lens computes ageing bands, direction and confidence, but a promise made this morning and one carried 14 days render as the same card at the same size.

## What's Working

1. **Named-rule discipline is real.** `.card:hover` gated behind `@media (hover: hover)`; the `*:focus-visible` block explains it must stay unlayered or 22 `focus:outline-none` utilities would beat it.
2. **The ADHD lens treats absence as information.** Empty analyses collapse to one sentence; the done-checkbox uses two glyphs rather than opacity because `opacity-40` measured 2.08:1 "on the one checklist this product exists to make tickable."
3. **`/help` documents confusions, not features.**

## Priority Issues

- **[P1] Stock light OpenStreetMap tiles inside a dark-only app.** `MeetingMap.tsx:68`, `LocationPicker.tsx:72`. ~240px of daylight above the ADHD analysis on mobile. Pins, popups and attribution were themed with care; the tiles — 90% of the pixels — were not. Fix: CARTO `dark_all`, shared constant, plus a height cap on the conversation page.
- **[P1] The A–Z letter rail overlaps the search input on mobile.** `people/page.tsx:560` `pr-9` (36px) vs a 52px rail under `(pointer: coarse)`, because globals.css inflates buttons to `min-width: 44px` on touch. 16px overlap; hit-testing at the rail's left edge returns the search input. Fix: pointer-scoped `pr-14`, plus a 24px floor on the letter buttons (they were 15.5×14px, failing WCAG 2.5.8).
- **[P1] Three visual dialects of one select-pill, and a spec that contradicts its code.** Solid copper (home filters, lens toggle), copper tint (commitments filters, SectionNav), and grey-on-grey with no copper at all (`/people` view modes, ~1.1:1). DESIGN.md's Parity Action rule still prescribes the no-op-handler behaviour that the code deliberately replaced with `disabled`. Frontmatter carries `#67e8f9` (stock cyan) and a dangling `{colors.paper}`.
- **[P2] No cost/duration estimate at `/analyze-group`.** `/rollup` (`estimateRun`) and `/conversation/[id]` both state it; the group run — the one whose size varies most — did not.
- **[P2] The home screen buries its own content and puts Restore one tap from the top.** 14 controls before the first conversation card at 375×812; no copper above the fold, so nothing is marked as the move; Backup/Restore/Refresh as three identical ghost buttons with the destructive one in the middle.

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (power user, one-handed, phone):** the letter rail bites him; shortcuts are desktop-only and navigation-only; `/commitments` has no bulk action. No redundant confirmations anywhere — credit where due.

**Jordan (first-timer):** four icon-only controls on `/people` below `sm`; "Web" is jargon; no copper primary above the fold; `/capture` strands him with "the store", a word appearing nowhere else in the UI.

**Sam (returning after 3 weeks):** structurally weakest. One day at a time, no unprocessed count, no date-range bulk path. Rebuilding a 3-week rollup chain is 21 sequential manual runs. `/commitments` — oldest-first, aged into bands — is the one thing that serves him well.

## Minor Observations

- `/capture` had no `layout.tsx`, so it was the only route without its own title.
- `/conversation/[id]` set one static tab title for every conversation.
- `AdhdResults` handled the fully-empty case well but shipped up to four "None." cards in the partially-empty case.
- `ConfirmDialog` body (the explanation of an irreversible action) was `text-slate-400`, the quietest colour, under a `text-white` title; and used `aria-label` duplicating a rendered `<h3>` instead of `aria-labelledby`/`aria-describedby`.
- `layout.tsx` `<body className="bg-slate-950">` was dead — globals.css's unlayered body rule always wins.
- `SectionNav` — 9 chips in an `overflow-x-auto` strip with no fade for the ~4 off-screen.
- `/help`'s inline prose links measure ~29px tall on mobile. NOT a defect: WCAG 2.5.8 exempts inline targets and globals.css documents `padding-block` as the deliberate compromise.
- Corrected from the review: the home search placeholder already reads "Search this list by title or topic…", and the search deliberately bypasses day-scoping — so renaming it to "Filter this day's list…" would have been wrong.

## Questions to Consider

1. Why is the home screen a conversation list at all? Neither of the product's two jobs is "browse chronologically" — that's the data model surfacing as the interface.
2. The app knows a promise is 14 days old and colours it clay — why is that the only thing that changes? What if the card aged?
3. The regenerate dialog warns that downstream chained days won't update — so why does the user own the chain rather than being offered a rebuild?
